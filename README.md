# Task-1
MARKET RESEARCH: DIGITAL PAYMENTS & UPI IN INDIA

SWYNEX – Task 1 | Target Market Research
Research period: 2025–2026 | Prepared using public sources

Prepared for academic submission. This document uses publicly available secondary sources.

1. Executive Summary

India’s digital-payments market has moved from an emerging technology segment to a mainstream consumer and merchant payment infrastructure. This research focuses on the target market for digital payment services, with UPI as the central use case.

The market is broad: individual consumers use digital payments for peer-to-peer transfers, shopping, food, bills and services, while merchants use them to accept payments and manage transactions. The strongest measurable indicator is transaction activity. NPCI reported 24,508.96 million UPI transactions in August 2026, worth ₹29,82,355.95 crore, with 752 banks live on UPI. These figures show the scale of monthly usage rather than the revenue earned by payment companies.

The research identifies convenience, smartphone/internet access, merchant acceptance, interoperability and expansion beyond major cities as important adoption drivers. At the same time, customers continue to care about transaction reliability, fraud awareness, privacy, customer support and ease of use.

2. Research Objective

The objective is to define the customer profile, identify market-size indicators, and explain major trends relevant to digital payment services in India. The analysis is based on publicly available secondary research from NPCI, RBI-related reporting, IAMAI and IBEF.

3. Target Customer
Primary consumer segments:
Students and young adults: frequent small-value payments, peer-to-peer transfers, food, travel and online purchases.
Salaried professionals: everyday merchant payments, bill payments, subscriptions and transfers.
Small-business owners and local merchants: QR-based collections, transaction records and digital acceptance.
Consumers in Tier-2/Tier-3 cities and towns: growing access to smartphones, internet services and digital financial infrastructure.
Digital-first shoppers: users who prefer online checkout and app-based payments.
Customer needs:
Fast and convenient payment experience
Wide merchant acceptance
Interoperability across banks and apps
Reliable transaction confirmation
Clear transaction history and support
Strong security and fraud-awareness features
4. Market Size Indicators
1) Monthly UPI activity

NPCI reports 24,508.96 million transactions in August 2026, with transaction value of ₹29,82,355.95 crore and 752 banks live on UPI.

2) FY2025 scale

RBI data reported through IBEF indicates UPI processed 185.8 billion transactions in FY2025, up from 131.1 billion in FY2024. UPI represented 83.4% of India’s payment transaction volume in FY2025.

3) Digital-payment ecosystem

IBEF reported that total digital-payment volume rose 37% year-on-year to 206 billion in FY2025, with value rising 30%, based on PwC’s India Payments Handbook 2025–2030.

4) Merchant and consumer reach

A 2025 government document reported around 460 million UPI individuals and 65 million merchants at that time. Because these figures come from a different date than the August 2026 NPCI statistics, they should be treated as a dated reach indicator rather than a current user count.

Important interpretation: transaction volume/value are market-activity indicators. They should not be described as the revenue or profit of the UPI ecosystem.

5. Key Market Trends
Trend 1 – UPI remains central to retail digital payments

RBI-related reporting shows UPI accounted for 83.4% of payment transaction volume in FY2025. This indicates the importance of UPI within India’s retail digital-payment ecosystem.

Trend 2 – High-frequency, small-value payments

UPI is heavily used for everyday transactions. NPCI’s August 2025 data shows person-to-merchant (P2M) UPI volume of 12,705.16 million versus 7,303.15 million person-to-person (P2P) transactions, highlighting the importance of merchant payments.

Trend 3 – Expansion of digital acceptance

Digital-payment acceptance has expanded among local and smaller merchants. Government reporting in 2025 cited 4.77 crore digital payment acceptance points supported through the Payments Infrastructure Development Fund as of May 31, 2025.

Trend 4 – Growth beyond major metros

Industry reporting points to increasing adoption in Tier-2 and smaller cities, supported by smartphone/internet penetration and expanding merchant infrastructure.

Trend 5 – More digital features and use cases

The ecosystem increasingly supports bill payments, QR payments, online checkout, recurring mandates and other financial use cases. This broadens the customer journey beyond a simple person-to-person money transfer.

Trend 6 – Security and trust remain essential

As transaction volumes increase, payment providers need strong authentication, fraud-awareness, reliable transaction status information and responsive customer support.

6. Customer Pain Points
Failed or delayed transactions can create uncertainty.
Fraud and social-engineering scams can reduce trust.
Users may find dispute/refund processes confusing.
Poor connectivity can affect the payment experience.
Some users need simple interfaces and local-language support.
Merchants may need easy reconciliation and clear transaction records.
Privacy and responsible handling of financial data remain important.
7. Competitive Landscape

The market includes banks, third-party UPI applications, payment-service providers and merchant-payment platforms. NPCI’s August 2025 ecosystem data listed major payer-side applications including PhonePe, Google Pay and Paytm, alongside other apps and bank applications.

This research does not rank these providers. Their competitive dimensions can instead be compared through:

User experience and reliability
Merchant acceptance
Feature breadth
Customer support
Security and fraud controls
Brand reach
Integration with banking and commerce services
8. Opportunities for New or Existing Services

Potential areas for product development include:

Simpler payment experiences for first-time or less digitally confident users
Better merchant dashboards and reconciliation tools
Fraud-warning and scam-awareness features
Local-language customer education
Payment tools designed for small businesses
Improved support for recurring and bill payments
Financial-management features built around transaction history

These are market opportunities identified from customer needs and ecosystem trends; they are not forecasts of commercial success.

9. Risks and Challenges
Cyber fraud and social engineering
Service outages or connectivity problems
Regulatory and compliance requirements
Customer trust and privacy concerns
Intense competition among payment providers
Dependence on banking, telecom and digital infrastructure
Changes in pricing, incentives or payment rules
10. Conclusion

India’s digital-payment market shows substantial and continuing usage, with UPI forming a major part of retail payment activity. The target market includes both consumers and merchants, with use cases ranging from everyday peer transfers to frequent merchant payments.

For a digital-payment service, the core customer expectations are convenience, reliability, security, broad acceptance and simple support. Market research should therefore track both quantitative indicators—transaction volume, value, banks and merchants—and qualitative factors such as trust, usability and customer pain points.

11. Research Methodology

This is secondary market research. Information was collected from public sources and compared by date and definition. Where sources report different periods or definitions, the dates are stated rather than combining them into a single figure. Transaction volume and transaction value are treated as market-activity indicators, not as company revenue.
